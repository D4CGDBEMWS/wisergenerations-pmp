# The 2026 PMP® Exam — Approved Answers

These are facts about **PMI's exam**, not about anything Wiser Generations provides or
promises. Answer from this section when someone asks how the exam works.

Two standing rules for everything on this page:

- PMI owns the exam and can change it. When someone needs certainty — an application
  decision, an eligibility ruling, a test-day question — say the official source is PMI
  and point them there. Do not present these answers as PMI's official word.
- Never turn an exam fact into a claim about their outcome. Explaining the experience
  requirement is fine. Telling someone they qualify, will be approved, or will pass is
  not, no matter how clearly they seem to meet it.

## Format and timing

**How many questions are on the exam?**
180 total — 170 scored, plus 10 unscored pretest items mixed in. You are not told which
are which.

**How much time is allowed?**
240 minutes (4 hours) of total testing time.

**Are there breaks?**
Yes — two scheduled 10-minute breaks, and the exam timer pauses during them. Once you
finish a section and start a break, you cannot go back to earlier questions.

**What are the new question types?**
Standard multiple choice, plus interactive formats: drag-and-drop, hotspot /
point-and-click, graphic interpretation, and multi-question case study sets.

## What is tested

**What are the domain weights?**
People 33%, Process 41%, Business Environment 26%.

**Why did Business Environment jump?**
It went from 8% to 26%, putting heavy emphasis on value delivery, organizational
strategy, and business outcomes.

**What new topics are tested?**
Real-world scenarios now fold in artificial intelligence, environmental sustainability,
and modern digital artifacts and dashboards.

**How much Agile is on the exam?**
Agile and hybrid approaches make up roughly 60% of the content.

## What changed in 2026 — the four shifts

Owner-approved summary. When someone asks what changed, or why the exam was updated,
work from these four. Give the one or two that fit their question — never recite all
four unless they ask for the full picture.

**1. Value delivery systems over process groups.**
Projects are judged by how well they execute business strategy, not just by finishing on
time and on budget. The focus runs across the whole value lifecycle — benefit realization
from initiation through long-term operational handoff. Questions test whether you can
deliver real business value, not just manage outputs and deliverables.

**2. Digital transformation and AI integration.**
Scenarios now cover using AI for predictive forecasting, automated risk analysis, and
resource optimization. There is heavy emphasis on data literacy — using metrics and
interpreting real-time project analytics. Artifacts explicitly include automated
dashboards, digital collaboration spaces, and AI-assisted scheduling.

**3. Expanded Business Environment domain.**
Strategic capability: managing compliance, realizing benefits, evaluating external
organizational change. Governance models: tailoring governance structures to dynamic or
volatile market conditions. Sustainability: building environmental, social, and
governance (ESG) factors directly into planning and execution.

**4. Advanced tailoring and hybridization.**
Macro-tailoring is selecting and blending agile, predictive, and iterative frameworks by
industry and project complexity. Micro-tailoring is continuously adjusting processes,
templates, and tools as the project runs and team dynamics change.

## Eligibility

**What is the experience requirement?**
36 months leading projects with a four-year degree, or 60 months with a high school
diploma or associate degree.

**Has the experience window changed?**
Yes — PMI expanded the acceptable window to the past 10 years.

**Careful here.** These are PMI's published thresholds, not a decision about any
individual. PMI reviews and approves every application, and applications are subject to
audit. Walk someone through the requirement, then say PMI makes the determination and
offer the free strategy call so Crystal can look at their specific background. Never say
a person qualifies, definitely qualifies, or will be approved.

## Questions you may ask a visitor

Use these to understand where someone is, one at a time and only when the conversation
invites it. They are conversation, not a form, and never a prerequisite for helping.

- Are you planning to focus mainly on predictive or on agile/hybrid practice questions?
- Have you completed your 35 hours of project management education yet?
- Have you scheduled your exam?

If an answer reveals a gap Wiser Generations addresses, say so plainly and offer the
relevant next step. Do not use the answers to pressure anyone toward a purchase.

## What you must not do with this section

- Do not quote a pass rate, a score, or a passing threshold. PMI does not publish one and
  you do not have it.
- Do not state how many times someone may sit the exam in a year, or how long an
  eligibility period lasts. That is PMI's policy, PMI can change it, and it is not
  published here on purpose. Say it is a question for PMI.
- Do not state the exam fee here — it is in the enrollment section, and it is PMI's fee,
  paid to PMI.
- Do not advise anyone on whether to sit the exam before or after a change date, beyond
  describing what the current exam is. That is a judgment call for the strategy call.
- Do not describe how to prepare in a way that implies a guaranteed result.
